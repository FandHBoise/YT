# Instagram Reels Script Writer — Worked Examples

Two complete examples showing the skill in action. Each one includes the creator's input, the full generated script, and a breakdown of why it's built the way it is.

---

## Example 1: Educational Reel — Cooking Tip

### Creator Input

```
Write me a 30-second Instagram Reel script.

Topic: Why your pasta always comes out soggy (and how to fix it in one step)
Niche: Home cooking for beginners
Goal: Saves
Format: Talking head with hands-on demo
Audience: People who cook at home but don't have formal training — they follow recipes but don't understand the "why"
CTA: Save this
```

---

### Generated Script

**Goal:** Saves
**Format:** Talking head + hands-on demo
**Hook type:** The Contrarian Statement

---

**[0–3s] HOOK**
[VISUAL: Medium shot, you're holding a pot of pasta water over the sink — mid-drain. Look directly at camera.]
[AUDIO: "Rinsing your pasta is ruining it."]
[ON-SCREEN TEXT: "STOP RINSING YOUR PASTA" in bold caps]

**[3–8s] SETUP**
[VISUAL: Cut to close-up of soggy pasta on a plate. Then cut back to talking head.]
[AUDIO: "Most home cooks rinse pasta after draining. It washes off the starch. That starch is what makes sauce cling."]

**[8–20s] BODY**
[VISUAL: Demo shot — drain pasta directly into a colander, no rinse. Then add pasta straight into a pan with sauce. Toss together. Close-up of sauce coating the noodles.]
[AUDIO: "Drain it, don't rinse it. Toss it straight into your sauce on low heat. See how the sauce grabs? That's the starch doing its job."]
[ON-SCREEN TEXT: "No rinse → sauce sticks" appears as you toss]

**[20–28s] PAYOFF**
[VISUAL: Side-by-side plate comparison — dull rinsed pasta on left, glossy sauced pasta on right.]
[AUDIO: "One habit. Completely different result. Your pasta's been good this whole time — you've just been washing the flavor off."]

**[28–30s] CTA**
[VISUAL: Back to talking head. Point at camera.]
[AUDIO: "Save this so you don't forget."]
[ON-SCREEN TEXT: "SAVE THIS" with a bookmark icon]

---

### Caption (for the Post)

Rinsing your pasta is the one mistake nobody talks about.

When you rinse cooked pasta, you're washing off surface starch — and that starch is the reason sauce sticks. Without it, even the best sauce slides right off.

Fix: drain your pasta, skip the rinse, and toss it straight into your sauce on low heat for 30–60 seconds. The starch acts like glue. Your sauce coats every strand instead of pooling at the bottom.

This works with marinara, carbonara, cream sauces, pesto — anything. It's the one thing culinary school teaches on day one that home cooks almost never hear.

Try it tonight and let me know in the comments if it changed anything.

#cookingtips #pastalovers #homecooking #kitchentips #foodhacks

### On-Screen Text Callouts
1. "STOP RINSING YOUR PASTA" — bold caps, appears at 0s
2. "No rinse → sauce sticks" — bold, appears during toss at ~14s
3. "SAVE THIS" + bookmark icon — bold, appears at 28s

### Suggested Audio
1. **Upbeat lo-fi kitchen beats** — something light with a simple rhythm; no lyrics that compete with your voiceover
2. **Trending "cooking reveal" audio** — search Instagram for "cooking hack" — the satisfying reveal audios pair well with side-by-side comparisons
3. **No audio / natural sound** — if your demo sounds good (sizzle, toss), lead with ambient kitchen sound and keep music low in the mix

---

### Why This Works

**The hook creates instant tension.** "Rinsing your pasta is ruining it" is a contrarian statement aimed at something almost every home cook does. Viewers who just rinsed pasta last night feel called out — that friction is what stops the scroll.

**The setup is 5 seconds, not 15.** The skill doesn't pad the middle. It gets to the demo fast because the demo IS the value.

**The CTA matches the goal.** The creator wanted saves, not follows. "Save this so you don't forget" is direct and gives the viewer a specific reason to save rather than a generic "save for later."

**The caption extends the value.** The first line mirrors the hook — it's what shows before the "more" cutoff. The body explains the "why," which earns the save from people who read captions.

## Example 2: Storytelling Reel — Business Lesson

### Creator Input

```
Write me a 60-second Instagram Reel script.

Topic: The client I turned down that taught me more about pricing than 3 years in business
Niche: Freelance design — I help other freelancers build sustainable businesses
Goal: Follows + profile visits
Format: Talking head, no demo
Audience: Freelancers earning $2,000–$5,000/month who feel stuck on pricing and undercharge
CTA: Follow for more
```

---

### Generated Script

**Goal:** Follows + profile visits
**Format:** Talking head
**Hook type:** The Before/After Teaser

---

**[0–3s] HOOK**
[VISUAL: Tight shot — shoulders up, direct eye contact with camera. Neutral background.]
[AUDIO: "I turned down a $12,000 project last year. It was the best decision I made in three years of freelancing."]
[ON-SCREEN TEXT: "I turned down $12,000" in bold — appears at 0s]

**[3–10s] SETUP**
[VISUAL: Stay on talking head. Slight lean in.]
[AUDIO: "A client came to me through a referral. Big brand, exciting brief, real budget. And I almost said yes out of habit."]

**[10–28s] BODY — The Problem**
[VISUAL: Talking head, natural hand gestures as you speak.]
[AUDIO: "Here's what I didn't see at first. Their timeline was half what I needed to do the work properly. They wanted full ownership of everything I created. And the revision process they described? Open-ended, no limit. I'd seen that before. That contract was worth $12,000 on paper — and maybe 400 hours of my life."]
[ON-SCREEN TEXT: "Red flag 1: impossible timeline" at ~13s]
[ON-SCREEN TEXT: "Red flag 2: unlimited revisions" at ~20s]

**[28–48s] BODY — The Lesson**
[VISUAL: Talking head, more deliberate pacing here. Let this breathe.]
[AUDIO: "I sent a counter-proposal. Better timeline, revision cap at three rounds, usage rights limited to their specific campaign. They passed. And that was the moment I understood something: clients who push back on boundaries before the contract is signed will push through them after. Saying no to that project opened up three weeks for work that was actually worth my time."]
[ON-SCREEN TEXT: "The right client respects your process" at ~40s]

**[48–57s] PAYOFF**
[VISUAL: Slight change — maybe sit back, slightly more relaxed energy.]
[AUDIO: "Your pricing problem is almost never your rates. It's which clients you're saying yes to. I was filling my calendar with the wrong work and wondering why I was exhausted."]

**[57–60s] CTA**
[VISUAL: Direct eye contact, back to tighter frame.]
[AUDIO: "Follow — I break down stuff like this every week."]
[ON-SCREEN TEXT: "Follow for more" with an arrow pointing up toward the follow button]

---

### Caption (for the Post)

I turned down $12,000 last year. It was the right call.

The client came through a referral. Good brand, real budget, exciting work. But when I read the brief carefully, the timeline was half what the project actually required, revisions were unlimited, and they wanted full ownership of every asset.

On paper: $12,000. In practice: months of scope creep and a contract I'd be trapped in.

I sent a counter-proposal with a realistic timeline, a 3-round revision cap, and campaign-specific usage rights. They passed.

Those three weeks went to a client who respected my process, paid on time, and became a referral source.

The pricing lesson nobody says out loud: you don't just price your work. You price your clients. Some projects cost more than they pay.

What's the most important thing you've learned about pricing? Drop it below — I read every comment.

#freelancedesign #freelancing #pricingadvice #creativebusiness #designbusiness

### On-Screen Text Callouts
1. "I turned down $12,000" — bold caps, 0s
2. "Red flag 1: impossible timeline" — appears ~13s
3. "Red flag 2: unlimited revisions" — appears ~20s
4. "The right client respects your process" — appears ~40s
5. "Follow for more" + upward arrow — appears 57s

### Suggested Audio
1. **Soft piano or acoustic background** — something emotionally neutral that doesn't compete with the story; no lyrics
2. **Lo-fi beats at low volume** — keeps the pacing feeling steady without distracting
3. **No music / natural voice only** — story Reels often perform well with no audio bed; the silence signals this is worth listening to

---

### Why This Works

**The hook answers "so what?" immediately.** Turning down money is counterintuitive — that's the tension. But the script doesn't leave viewers hanging: it tells them the outcome ("best decision I made") in the same breath, which turns it from a tease into a promise.

**The story has a clear structure.** Setup → problem discovery → decision → lesson → payoff. Each section is about 15–20 seconds. The viewer always knows where they are in the story.

**On-screen text reinforces, it doesn't repeat.** The text callouts name the red flags explicitly while the audio explains them. This creates a layered experience — viewers who watch with sound AND viewers who watch muted both get the full point.

**The CTA earns itself.** The skill doesn't ask for a follow until the creator has delivered a specific, useful insight. "Follow — I break down stuff like this every week" works because the viewer has just seen what "stuff like this" means. The value case for following is already made.

**The caption does different work than the video.** The Reel tells the story. The caption adds the business-lesson summary and ends with a question to drive comments — two different engagement behaviors from the same piece of content.

---

## Key Takeaways

**What these examples have in common:**

1. **The hook is specific, not vague.** "Rinsing your pasta is ruining it" and "I turned down $12,000" are concrete. Generic hooks like "Let me share something important" don't stop scrolls.

2. **Every section has a job.** Hook stops the scroll. Setup earns 8 more seconds. Body delivers on the promise. CTA converts attention into action. Nothing is decorative.

3. **On-screen text supports the audio — it doesn't duplicate it.** If your text says exactly what you're saying, it's redundant. Use text to emphasize, label, or give the muted-viewer version.

4. **The CTA matches one specific goal.** The cooking Reel wanted saves — the CTA asks for saves. The business Reel wanted follows — the CTA asks for follows. Never ask for both.

5. **Captions extend value, not length.** The caption isn't a transcript of the video. It's a companion piece that adds context, earns the save or the comment, and targets Instagram's search algorithm.
