# YouTube Chapter Generator — Worked Examples

---

## Example 1: 20-Minute Tutorial Video

### Creator Input

> My video is about setting up a home recording studio on a budget. It's 19 minutes long. Here's my outline:
>
> - Intro and what we're building (about 1.5 min)
> - Gear you actually need vs. what's optional (4 min)
> - Room setup and acoustic treatment on the cheap (4 min)
> - Microphone placement tips (3 min)
> - Recording software — free vs. paid options (3.5 min)
> - Quick demo: recording a voiceover (2 min)
> - Final thoughts and what to upgrade first (1 min)

### Generated Chapters

```
0:00 Intro — budget home studio setup
1:30 Gear you actually need (and what to skip)
5:30 Room setup and cheap acoustic treatment
9:30 Microphone placement tips
12:30 Recording software: free vs. paid
16:00 Demo: recording a voiceover
18:00 What to upgrade first
```

### Why This Works

**Clear viewer intent in chapter 2:** "Gear you actually need (and what to skip)" tells the viewer exactly what they'll get from that section. A viewer who has already bought gear but hasn't set up their room can jump straight to chapter 3.

**Keywords up front:** "Budget home studio setup" in the intro chapter echoes the video's likely search terms. "Acoustic treatment" and "recording software" appear naturally in mid-video chapters where viewers searching for those specific topics will find them.

**The demo gets its own chapter:** Even at only 2 minutes long, giving the demo a dedicated chapter lets viewers who want to see a real-world example jump directly there without scrubbing.

**No filler titles:** "Room setup and cheap acoustic treatment" is specific. A title like "Setting Up Your Space" would be too vague for someone scanning chapters.

## Example 2: 45-Minute Podcast / Interview Video

### Creator Input

> This is a 44-minute interview with a freelance video editor who switched from agency work to solo client work. Here's the rough transcript structure:
>
> [0:00–2:10] Me introducing the guest and the topic
> [2:10–8:45] Her background — film school, agency years, why she left
> [8:45–16:20] How she got her first freelance clients (cold email + referrals)
> [16:20–24:00] Pricing: how she figured out her rates and her first big mistake
> [24:00–31:15] A difficult client story and how she handled it
> [31:15–37:40] Tools she uses: project management, contracts, invoicing
> [37:40–42:00] Advice for editors thinking about going freelance
> [42:00–44:00] Where to find her and wrap-up

### Generated Chapters

```
0:00 Intro
2:10 From film school to agency work — her path to freelance
8:45 How she landed her first freelance clients
16:20 Setting rates: what she got wrong at first
24:00 Handling a difficult client (real story)
31:15 Tools for running a freelance editing business
37:40 Advice for editors thinking about going solo
42:00 Where to find her
```

### Why This Works

**Each chapter represents a complete idea:** The interview covers 7 distinct topics. Each gets one chapter. There is no need to split "Handling a difficult client" into "The situation" and "The resolution" — viewers want to watch that section start to finish.

**The titles tease without spoiling:** "Setting rates: what she got wrong at first" tells the viewer there is a mistake story inside, which is more compelling than "Freelance pricing." It respects the viewer's time by telling them what they'll learn.

**Interview-appropriate length:** At 44 minutes with 7 chapters, each chapter averages about 6 minutes — a natural segment length for interview content. Splitting further would make the chapter list feel cluttered.

**The outro is short but present:** At 2 minutes, the closing segment is worth its own chapter so viewers who want to follow the guest know exactly where to find that information.

**No timestamp at an awkward mid-sentence point:** The transcript showed the "difficult client" segment beginning with a natural question at 24:00, so that is where the chapter starts, not at 23:47 where the previous answer was still trailing off.

---

## Key Takeaways

1. **More detail in your input = more accurate timestamps.** A full transcript beats a rough outline, which beats a one-line description.

2. **Chapter count should match content complexity.** A 20-minute tutorial with 7 clear steps needs 7 chapters. A 20-minute rambling vlog might only need 4.

3. **Titles are navigation tools, not marketing copy.** Write them the way a viewer mid-video would describe what they want to find.

4. **You can always revise.** Give the AI your chapters and say "chapter 4 title feels too vague" or "the pricing section actually starts 2 minutes later" — it will adjust without regenerating everything.
